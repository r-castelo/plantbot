Plantbot Build Guide

Wire up a talking, face-pulling plant buddy with an ESP32-S3, four sensors, an e-paper face and a speaker. No breadboard, no circuit board: every part connects with soldered cables that plug straight onto the ESP32.

  • ⏱ 3–4 hours
  • Level: first-time solderer
  • ≈ 40 solder joints
  • Unplug-able: the ESP32 stays removable

1 · The plan in one picture

The ESP32 is the brain. It has rows of metal pins on both long edges. Every other part reaches it through a wire that ends in a small black female plug (a "Dupont" connector) that pushes onto one pin. You will solder only where wires have to join: three sensors share the same four wires, the speaker needs leads, and the MP3 player gets a resistor and a capacitor.

Why plugs on the ESP32 and solder everywhere else?

Solder joints don't wiggle loose, so the joins between wires are soldered. The ESP32 end uses plugs so you can pull the brain out to reflash or replace it, and fix a wrong pin by just moving a plug.

3 · Soldering school: three skills

This whole build uses only three moves. Practise each one twice on scrap wire before you touch the real parts. Ten minutes of practice saves an hour of redoing joints.

Safety first

The tip is about 350 °C. Always park the iron in its stand, never on the table. Work near an open window or a small fan, because flux smoke is irritating. Unplug the iron when you take a break.

Skill A · Strip and tin a wire

"Tinning" means coating the bare copper with a thin layer of solder. Tinned wires join in a second.

  1. Strip 6 mm of insulation. Don't nick the copper strands. If a few strands get cut, cut the end off and strip again.
  2. Twist the strands tightly clockwise between your fingers so no strays stick out.
  3. Heat and feed. Hold the iron tip under the copper for 1 second, then touch the solder to the copper on the other side, not to the iron. The solder soaks into the strands like water into a sponge.
  4. Check: the strands should look silver and slightly shiny, still showing their twisted texture, and not be a fat blob.

Skill B · Join two wires and cover the joint

  1. Slide heat-shrink on first! Cut 15 mm of tubing and push it onto one wire, far away from the joint. Forgetting this is the #1 beginner mistake. Everyone does it once.
  2. Tin both wire ends (Skill A).
  3. Lay the two tinned ends side by side, overlapping about 5 mm, and hold them still with helping hands or Blu-Tack.
  4. Touch the iron to the overlap for 2–3 seconds. The tinning on both wires melts together. Add a tiny bit of fresh solder if needed.
  5. Count to 5 without moving the wires while the joint cools.
  6. Slide the tubing over the joint and shrink it with the heat gun.

Skill C · Solder a wire to a pin, hole or tab

  1. Tin the wire. Also tin the tab or pad: heat it and touch solder to it until a small pool forms.
  2. Hold the wire against the tab (or push it through the hole), heat both together, and let the solder flow around the wire.
  3. A good joint looks like a smooth little volcano or fillet. A ball sitting on top means the pad wasn't hot enough, so reheat it.

4 · Make plug-ended wires ("pigtails")

A pigtail is half a jumper wire: a female plug on one end and bare wire on the other. Almost every connection to the ESP32 starts as a pigtail.

1

Cut 8 pigtails

Cut 4 jumpers in half. Pick colours that match their job so the wiring stays readable: red = 3V3, black = GND, white = SDA, yellow = SCL, orange = 5V, plus any 3 others for the MP3 player.

2

Strip and tin the cut ends

Skill A: strip 6 mm, twist, tin.

3

Flag every pigtail now

Wrap a little masking-tape flag right behind the plug and write where it goes, for example J1-1 3V3. Flags written now save you from guessing later.

How to read pin names in this guide

Hold the ESP32 with the USB ports pointing down and the chip facing you. The left pin row is J1 and the right one is J3, each counted 1 to 22 from the top. So J1-1 is the top-left pin (3V3) and J1-22 is the bottom-left pin (GND), right next to the USB ports. The full map is in section 9.

5 · Build the sensor harness

The light, temperature and proximity sensors all talk over one shared pair of wires called I²C (SDA = data, SCL = clock), and each also needs power (3V3) and ground (GND). So you join like with like: all GNDs together, all 3V3s together, all SDAs together, all SCLs together. The moisture sensor joins only the 3V3 and GND groups.

Plan lengths first

Put the parts where they'll live. The light sensor goes on top, facing the sky, and nothing should shade it. The proximity sensor faces forward, toward where people walk up. Keep the temperature sensor at least 5 cm away from the ESP32, which runs a few degrees warm and would make your plant "feel" hotter than it is. Measure each sensor's distance to where the ESP32 will sit, add 5 cm, and cut wires to that length.

1

Cut the two Grove cables

Take one Grove cable per Grove sensor (AHT20 and VL53L0X). Cut it to length, keeping the plug end that goes into the sensor. Strip and tin the 4 cut wires. Grove colours are always the same: black GND, red VCC, white SDA, yellow SCL.

2

Solder 4 wires onto the BH1750 light sensor

The Adafruit board has a row of holes. Use Skill C to solder a wire into VIN, GND, SDA and SCL: push the wire through from the top and solder on the back. Leave 3Vo and ADDR empty.

Alternative: plug a STEMMA QT / Qwiic cable into the side socket and cut its far end. That's no soldering on the sensor, but the wires are very thin.

3

Prepare the moisture sensor cable

The sensor comes with a 3-wire cable that has plugs on the far end. Check the labels printed on the sensor to identify the wires (usually black = GND, red = VCC, yellow or blue = AOUT). Keep the plug on AOUT, because it goes straight onto the ESP32. Cut the plugs off VCC and GND, then strip and tin those two wires.

4

Make the four splices: many wires into one

Each splice joins one pigtail with all its matching sensor wires:

SpliceWires going inPigtail out to ESP32
GNDBH1750 GND, AHT20 black, VL53L0X black, moisture GNDJ3-1 GND
3V3BH1750 VIN, AHT20 red, VL53L0X red, moisture VCCJ1-1 3V3
SDABH1750 SDA, AHT20 white, VL53L0X whiteJ1-12 GPIO 8
SCLBH1750 SCL, AHT20 yellow, VL53L0X yellowJ1-15 GPIO 9

For a many-wire splice, use a pigtail twist:

↳
  1. Slide a 25 mm piece of 6 mm heat-shrink onto the pigtail first.
  2. Gather the tinned ends so they all point the same way, with the tips lined up.
  3. Twist them together like a rope for 8–10 mm.
  4. Heat the twist from underneath and feed solder from the top until it soaks all the way through.
  5. Trim the tip to 6 mm and fold it flat along the wires. Shrink the tubing over it.
Stagger the splices

Place the four splices 3 cm apart along the bundle, not all at one point. Side by side they'd make a fat lump, and bare spots could touch. Staggered, they make a slim cable.

5

Tidy the harness

Put a zip tie or a strip of tape every 5 cm. Check that the flag on each pigtail is still readable.

6 · Wire the MP3 player & speaker

The DFPlayer Mini is the stamp-sized board with a microSD slot. It plays the voice clips and drives the speaker by itself. It needs power (5V and GND), two data wires, one "busy" wire, the speaker and a capacitor.

Never connect a speaker wire to GND

The DFPlayer's amplifier pushes on both speaker pins. Connect the speaker only between SPK1 and SPK2. Connecting either one to ground can kill the amplifier.

1

Solder the capacitor onto the player

The capacitor has a minus stripe (a row of "–" marks) on its side, and the long leg is plus. Bend the legs so the plus leg touches the VCC pin and the minus leg touches the GND pin on the same side (the 7th pin down). Solder each leg to its pin right at the module, where the pin comes through the board, so the plugs still fit below. Slip 2.5 mm heat-shrink over whichever leg runs past the other pins, so it can't touch them.

2

Make the resistor jumper (ESP32 → player RX)

Take two pigtails. Shorten the 1 kΩ resistor's legs to 8 mm each. Solder one pigtail to each leg (Skill B), then cover the whole resistor with heat-shrink. You get one cable with a plug at both ends and the resistor hidden in the middle. Flag it J1-10 GPIO 17 on one end and DFP RX on the other.

3

Prepare the speaker leads

If your speaker already has wires with a small white plug, cut the plug off. If it has bare metal tabs, solder about 25 cm of red and black wire to them (Skill C). Then solder a pigtail onto the other end of each speaker wire, so the speaker ends in two plugs. Flag them SPK1 and SPK2.

With a single speaker, it doesn't matter which wire goes to SPK1 and which to SPK2.

4

Use plain jumpers for the rest

Four uncut female–female jumpers go between the player and the ESP32:

The VCC and GND plugs fit on the pins even with the capacitor soldered there, because you soldered it right at the module.

My player has no pins, only holes

Then skip the plugs on that side. Solder the pigtails' bare ends straight into the player's holes (Skill C) and solder the capacitor legs into the same VCC and GND holes.

7 · Connect the e-paper (no soldering)

Your Waveshare 1.54" V2 (Rev 2.1) came with a cable that has a white connector on one end and 8 loose plugs on the other. Push the white connector into the display. The 8 plugs go straight onto the ESP32.

Is there a 9th wire labelled PWR?

The Rev 2.1 boards we've seen have 8 pins. If yours has a 9th labelled PWR, plug it onto J1-5 GPIO 5. The firmware will switch it on. Without power on that pin, the display stays blank.

8 · Test before you plug in

Five minutes with a multimeter catches almost every wiring mistake before it can damage anything. Set the meter to the beep (continuity) mode, the one with the little sound-wave symbol.

  • Each splice beeps through. From the GND pigtail's plug, beep to the GND of every sensor (BH1750 GND pad, the black wire in each Grove plug, the moisture sensor GND). Repeat for 3V3, SDA and SCL.
  • No beep between different groups. 3V3 pigtail ↔ GND pigtail must be silent. So must SDA ↔ SCL, SDA ↔ GND and SCL ↔ GND. A beep here means two splices touch or a strand is poking through. Fix it before going on.
  • Resistor jumper: switch the meter to Ω. It should read about 1 kΩ (0.95–1.05 k) end to end.
  • Player power: VCC pin ↔ GND pin must not beep. A short beep that fades away is OK, though: that's the capacitor charging.
  • Speaker: SPK1 ↔ SPK2 plugs read about 4 Ω.

9 · Plug everything in

Unplug the USB cable first. Then push each plug onto its pin, working down the list. Plugs go fully on, so you shouldn't see any metal between the plug and the black pin strip.

Mounting the ESP32

The pins stick out of the bottom, so the board can't lie flat. Either stand it on two strips of thick double-sided foam tape along its edges, which gives enough room under it for the plugs, or mount it upside down with the pins pointing up. After everything works, put a small dab of hot glue on the side of each row of plugs to stop them from wiggling off. Hot glue peels off cleanly later.

10 · Waterproof, SD card, first boot

Seal the moisture sensor

These sensors die when water creeps into the electronics at the top. Paint the edges of the board and the area around the electronics with two coats of clear nail polish, letting it dry between coats. Then put a piece of 12 mm heat-shrink over the electronics. Only push the sensor into the soil up to the white line, never deeper.

Prepare the microSD card

Format the card as FAT32. Make numbered folders 01, 02, … with files named 001.mp3, 002.mp3, …. We'll generate the actual voice clips together in the next phase. For a first test, put any MP3 at /01/001.mp3.

First power-on

  1. Double-check the three power pins: J1-21 5V goes only to the player's VCC. J1-1 and J1-2 (3V3) go only to the harness and the display.
  2. Plug the USB cable into the ESP32's port marked USB (the one next to the J3 side) and into the charger.
  3. The red power LED lights up. Nothing gets hot. Touch the sensors and the player chip for a few seconds to check: warm is fine, too hot to touch is not. If anything gets hot, unplug immediately and re-run the tests in section 8.
  4. Done! The hardware is ready for the firmware.

11 · If something doesn't work

SymptomLikely causeFix
Solder balls up and won't stickDirty tip, or the wire or pad isn't hot enoughWipe the tip in the brass wool and re-tin it. Hold the iron on the joint 1 second longer before feeding solder.
Insulation melts back far from the jointThe iron stayed on too longHeat for 2–3 seconds at most, and tin the wires first so joints need less heat.
Board doesn't show up on the computerA plug on J1-13/14 (strapping pins), or a short on 3V3Unplug everything, re-test, and plug the parts back one group at a time.
A sensor is missing in the I²C scanBroken strand in a splice, or SDA and SCL swapped at one sensorBeep-test that sensor's wires from its plug to the pigtails.
The board reboots when the speaker gets loudWeak USB supply, or the capacitor is missingUse a 2 A charger and keep the volume ≤ 20/30. Check the capacitor's polarity.
Loud hiss or buzz from the speakerThe 1 kΩ resistor is missing, or a GND plug is looseUse the resistor jumper and push the GND plugs fully on.
E-paper stays blankWrong pin order, or the PWR pin isn't connectedCheck against the e-paper table. On 9-pin boards, PWR goes on J1-5.
